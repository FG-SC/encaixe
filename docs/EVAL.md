## 2. Drift: the preference changes on a known day

30 simulated users over 40 days on 68 open jobs. On day 20, 15 users change their preferred area; 7 of them also save a relevant revision (a new dated experience in the new area), the others change taste only. Quality is measured on the full top 10 against the current oracle (the simulation's hidden utility).

![precision all](eval/drift_precision_all.svg)

![precision drifters](eval/drift_precision_drifters.svg)

Precision@10 of the users whose preference changed:

| Variant | 7 days before | 3 days right after | last 5 days | days to recover 90% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| static-v1 | 0.424 | 0.194 | 0.194 | not within 20 days |
| feedback-v1 | 0.469 | 0.131 | 0.200 | not within 20 days |
| feedback-v1 sem decaimento | 0.468 | 0.125 | 0.166 | not within 20 days |
| feedback-v1 sem desconto por revisão | 0.469 | 0.133 | 0.191 | not within 20 days |

**Reading:** before the change, learning from swipes helps (0.469 vs 0.424). Right after it, `feedback-v1` falls further than the static ranking (0.131 vs 0.194): old likes point the wrong way. At the end of the window it is above static (0.200 vs 0.194).
Without time decay the end-of-window precision is 0.166 (worse than with the 21-day half-life).

Caveat: the oracle is a weighted sum of area affinity, requirements, seniority, salary and arrangement, so any experiment built on it favours rankings that look like it (see the weighted baseline in the replay).
