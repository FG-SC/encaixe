# Decisions

Choices made where the product spec was silent or open to interpretation, and every deviation from it. Each one is also reflected in `app/config.py` and, when it affects users, on the rules page.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unknown and not-applicable criterion values are imputed with the median of the known values, then ranked with everyone else. | The spec says an unknown value gets the median position and "stays in the middle"; imputing the median value does exactly that and keeps good disclosed salaries above and bad ones below. |
| 2 | Tie-break chain: final sum → Atende position → Requirements position → item id. | The spec gives the first two steps. Requirements next keeps evidence first (and makes the keyword-stuffing guarantee hold in symmetric cases); the id makes the order total and reproducible. |
| 3 | The timezone criterion is "not applicable" (neutral) for non-remote jobs. | The spec defines it only for remote jobs. |
| 4 | The modality filter has two reasons: modality not accepted, and onsite/hybrid beyond `max_distance_km`. | Each has a different adjustment shortcut in "Vagas que não te mostramos". |
| 5 | For the out-of-area filter, a candidate without proven skills uses the domains of their listed skills. | Otherwise every job would be out of area for someone with no dated experience yet. |
| 6 | A job without recognized must requirements is "not evaluable" and never counts as a good fit. | "Proves ≥ 80% of 0 requirements" would make good-fit counts meaningless. |
| 7 | The blind card shows company and institution names; only name, contact, photo and age wait for the match. | First built hiding them too (no brands in the first screening); the owner reverted it on 2026-10-20 because recruiters want to know which companies and schools. They are still never a ranking criterion (no prestige score). Hiding them again is a `blind_hidden` switch. |
| 8 | Weighted baseline: Interessa criteria weigh 1/3 each; with the history pillar, pillars weigh 0.6 / 0.25 / 0.15; the requirements tuple becomes k1 + k2/10 + k3/100 + k4/1000. | The spec gives only the Atende and pillar weights. It is a baseline for the evaluation, never served. |
| 9 | Seniority fallback by years (used only when titles say nothing): <2 → Júnior, 2–5 → Pleno, 5–9 → Sênior, 9–13 → Staff, 13+ → Principal; students → Estágio. | The spec says titles rule and years are only a fallback, without thresholds. |
| 10 | Events carry an extra `rules_hash` column. | So every recorded judgment can be traced to the exact rule set that produced the list. |
| 11 | Events are append-only, with one exception: the LGPD purge (`allow_event_delete`). | The spec requires both append-only events and the deletion of a candidate's events on request. |
| 12 | k-anonymity renders every count from 0 to 4 as "<5". | Literal reading of the spec ("aggregate with a count below 5 appears as <5"). |
| 13 | `Job.version` bumps only when the posting content changes; an LLM re-extraction updates the vectors of the same version. | A better extraction is not a new posting and must not trigger the "vaga atualizada" badge. |
| 14 | Embedding batch size is 32 instead of 64. | The development machine had about 1.4 GB of free RAM; the model's temporaries grow linearly with the batch (see research notes). |
| 15 | Vectors are mean-pooled, cast to float32 and L2-normalized by Encaixe. | The model outputs token states; fastembed 0.8.1 (the first backend) returned mean-pooled, non-normalized float64 vectors, and the onnx backend reproduces that pooling (#35). |
| 16 | ONNX Runtime's CPU memory arena is disabled (`enable_cpu_mem_arena=False`). | Measured on the dev machine: 605 MB after loading the model; embedding 256 texts in batches of 32 peaked at 815 MB with the arena and 613 MB without it (the arena keeps the batch buffers). Needed for the 700 MB steady-state budget. |
| 17 | When a job has a requirements section, skills found only in its title are desirable, not required. | The requirements list is what the company states; "ML Engineer" in a title would otherwise add a must that the posting never lists. |
| 18 | "Transversal" skills (Excel, Git, negotiation, project management…) define no domain and do not count as proof of area. | An analyst who uses Excel must not make a sales job "in area"; an analyst who builds sales dashboards does not prove sales. |
| 19 | Sentences stating an interest ("Interesse em machine learning") do not create listed skills. | Interest is not a claim of skill. |
| 20 | A Brazilian CV written in Portuguese gets Portuguese (native) as an inferred language. | CVs rarely list the mother tongue; it is shown as "inferido" for review. |
| 21 | Counterfactual skill order: zero-credit musts first, then the skill that unlocks most jobs, then the more specific one (deeper in the taxonomy), then alphabetical. | When several single changes work, "com PyTorch" is more actionable than "com deep learning". |
| 22 | Languages are required items for the requirement criterion and good fit, but never count as "proof of area" in the out-of-area filter. | A Brazilian proving Portuguese would otherwise never see an out-of-area job filtered. |
| 23 | The salary criterion uses the job's `comp_max`, or its `comp_min` when it is the only figure. | A single disclosed number is both the floor and the top of the range. |
| 24 | **Greenhouse, Lever and GitHub are on by the owner's decision (2026-10-20)**; they first shipped disabled. `--accept-terms` still runs a source that is off. | The spec turns them on by default but also says: "if you don't find clear permission, leave the source off and record it". The terms check (2026-10-19, independently verified) found public endpoints and no prohibition, but no explicit permission to aggregate and redisplay third-party postings (GitHub: "ambiguous, leaning no", and issues carry recruiters' personal data, which are stripped). The agent left them off and reported it; Felipe turned them on, and the rules page says so. We Work Remotely forbids this use outright and stays off. |
| 25 | GitHub issue bodies are used for extraction but never redisplayed; contacts are stripped before storage. | GitHub's licence covers display "through the Service"; showing title, labels, extracted fields and a link back is the defensible minimum, and it is what the communities' own README endorses (openings.dev links back to the issue). |
| 26 | Ingestion: serial requests, ≥ 1 s apart, once per board per 24 h, ETag revalidation, backoff on 429/5xx, never an application endpoint. | Lever's robots.txt asks for a 1 s crawl delay; the others document no limit, so the polite default applies to all. |
| 27 | The simulation's hidden utility (oracle) is a weighted sum of area affinity (0.45), requirements (0.25), seniority (0.15), salary (0.10) and arrangement (0.05), plus noise. | Any synthetic oracle encodes assumptions; this one is stated in the code and in docs/EVAL.md, including that it favours the weighted baseline. |
| 28 | In the drift experiment a preference change is a switch of the same breadth (new area 1.0, old area back to the 0.1 floor). | A first version kept the old area at 0.5: the taste became broader rather than different and every ranking "recovered" instantly — an artifact, caught in the curves and fixed. |
| 29 | Replay uses today's open jobs as the candidate set and up to 400 sampled sessions. | Rebuilding the exact past job set needs per-job open/close history; the approximation is stated in the report. |
| 30 | `encaixe bench` sums the RSS of the whole process tree. | A Windows venv's `python.exe` is a launcher that runs the real interpreter as a child; sampling only the launcher reports ~4 MB. |
| 31 | The spec text available to this build ended in §11 (screens, right after the Raio-X goal). The rest of §11 (other screens and the API list), §12 (Explorar), §13 (evaluation), §14–§15 (phases and stop points) and §16 (demo) were inferred from cross-references in §1–§11. | The owner accepted these inferences on 2026-10-20. The inferred behaviour is described in ARCHITECTURE.md (screens, Explorar, evaluation, demo story); stop points were taken as "stop only where a decision is genuinely the owner's" (sources' terms, #24). |
| 32 | The job index reads only the columns the ranking uses (descriptions, provenance and version snapshots stay on disk) and keeps one copy of each vector (rows of the matrix). | With 2,801 real postings the server's bench peak went from 665 MB to 737 MB; these two changes brought it to 722 MB, still over the 700 MB budget. The rest was the embedding model: 275 MB for the ONNX session and 252 MB for the `tokenizers` vocabulary of XLM-R (measured); the tokenizer change (#35) and the sample scope (#38) brought the server to 471 MB. |
| 33 | `encaixe eval oracle`: the static ranking scored against the simulation's oracle over all eligible jobs, next to the replay. | On real postings the replay's static AUC fell to 0.37: it only compares jobs the served ranker chose to show, and inside that band a lower static score means a higher history score. The unbiased check (0.70) shows it is the logging policy, and the report says so. |
| 34 | Tests never read the developer's `.env` (conftest replaces the reader). | A real `GROQ_API_KEY` in `.env` would otherwise turn the "LLM off" tests into calls (blocked by the no-network guard, but failing). |
| 35 | The embedding backend is `onnx`: the same quantized ONNX file fastembed used, on ONNX Runtime directly, tokenized with the model's own sentencepiece vocabulary instead of `tokenizers`. fastembed, tokenizers and five packages only they needed left the lock; `EMBEDDINGS=fastembed` still works as an alias. | Measured: the `tokenizers` XLM-R vocabulary held 249 MB, sentencepiece holds 38 MB. Token ids were identical on all 5,112 texts of the corpus (every open job and synthetic profile) and the vectors match fastembed's to cosine 1.000000 on 404 sampled texts, so the change is invisible to the ranking. Files are pinned by commit (config). The spec's remedies for a budget overrun were tfidf, smaller batches or recording the deviation; this keeps the model. |
| 36 | `beyond_distance` also covers jobs that do not say their arrangement (they count as onsite) and, without a known city, non-remote jobs in another country. | Real postings often omit the arrangement and name cities the list does not have; before this, a USD job in Canada with no arrangement passed every filter and topped a junior analyst's deck in Florianópolis. Remote jobs abroad are untouched (eligibility handles them), and the filtered list shows the reason. |
| 37 | The country of a location field is the last country it names ("City, Region, Country"); "British Columbia" and "New South Wales" are aliases of CA and AU. | "British" is also a GB demonym and "Wales" part of GB: "Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada" was read as GB. |
| 38 | **Scope: a sample of one region** (owner's decision, 2026-10-20, "keep it simple"): ingestion keeps onsite/hybrid jobs in Santa Catarina and remote jobs open to people in Brazil; the rest is not stored, only counted per board (`RULES.scope`, `app/ingest/scope.py`, rules page §10). Recruiter-published jobs and the fictional seed are not cut. | An MVP does not need national coverage. One region keeps the data small (659 of 2,733 real postings on 2026-10-20), matches the seed and personas (Florianópolis) and the curated city list, and keeps salaries mostly in BRL. Broader coverage (all Brazilian municipalities, more countries) was sketched and dropped. |
| 39 | **An undisclosed salary counts as paying exactly the candidate's minimum** (`RULES.criteria.salary_unknown_slack = 0`), an exception to #1's median imputation (owner's decision, 2026-10-20: "the conservative rule"). Other unknown values still get the median. | With real postings, the median of the disclosed salaries sat far above a typical minimum (international and annual figures), so an honest disclosure such as R$ 8,000 for a junior role ranked below every undisclosed job (Carla's job was 180th of 395 for Ana; with this rule it is 4th). Assuming the minimum is the cautious reading of silence, and transparency still pays when the salary is good. |
